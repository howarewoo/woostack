---
title: Verification
description: How evidence supports claims about a change and its delivery.
---

Verification connects a claim about a change to an observation that can support it. A passing test
may show that a particular behavior worked under its tested conditions. An agent's summary or a
status field can describe work, but neither proves that behavior or shows that a change was merged.

## Evidence fits the claim

Different claims need different observations. Source inspection can establish what code says, while
rendered behavior needs an observation of the running result. A local check does not establish remote
merge readiness. Each result has a scope: the source, inputs, environment, and behavior it actually
covered.

The appropriate evidence depends on the task and its consequences. Applicable user, project, and
workflow requirements still apply. For ordinary reversible changes, existing relevant checks and the
smallest practical observation can be enough. Additional checks need an applicable requirement or a
specific material risk. Consequential work involving privacy, authorization, destructive effects,
migration, recovery, or finances calls for stronger targeted evidence.

For example, a wording correction may need its claims and links inspected. A new documentation route
can also affect rendering and navigation, so source inspection alone may not cover the change. A
200% zoom check is not a default WooStack requirement or blocker; an explicit project or task
requirement can make it necessary. Other applicable accessibility and safety requirements still apply.

## Reuse and limits

Earlier evidence can remain useful when the relevant source, tests, fixtures, dependencies,
configuration, and environment still apply. An unrelated edit or a new revision identifier alone
does not invalidate it. Changed inputs or uncertain equivalence call for affected checks again.
Evidence for separate contributions also does not establish an untested interaction between them.

A failed, timed-out, cancelled, unavailable, or unrun required check leaves its affected boundary
unverified. A pre-existing failure remains a failure. Reporting the result and its limits helps the
next person distinguish an observed pass from missing proof.

## Delivery boundaries

A draft pull request makes a change visible for review. When its publication conditions permit it,
it may disclose incomplete implementation acceptance, but its existence does not establish that
acceptance. Implementation acceptance depends on the selected evidence.

Dependent work has its own prerequisite and contract evidence. Publishing a draft alone does not
release that work. Merge and release have separate requirements, and WooStack leaves merging,
auto-merge, and merge queues to humans. These boundaries are not a universal sequence: their actual
conditions depend on the task and workflow.

For the practical selection and reporting of checks, see
[Set verification requirements](/docs/guides/verification).
